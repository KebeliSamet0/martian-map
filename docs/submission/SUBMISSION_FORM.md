## High-level project summary
Martian Map is an astronaut EVA rehearsal simulator on real Mars terrain. On the Gale Crater / Mount Sharp boxwork area that Curiosity explored in 2025–2026, the astronaut compares three planned routes by time, energy, oxygen and radiation dose, then walks the chosen one in 3D while the life-support budget drains. The route is re-planned live from wherever the astronaut stands; a helmet camera shows Curiosity's real Navcam photo of that spot read by our AI4Mars-trained terrain model; a scanner marks caution / no-go ground and where the model has little evidence. Every number comes from NASA data, a published equation or a labelled planning assumption.

## Project details

**What it does.** Before a spacewalk the astronaut reads a mission briefing, picks one of three routes (shortest, balanced, safest) priced in minutes, kcal, litres of O₂ and µGy, and rehearses it in a 3D walk on the HiRISE terrain. Oxygen, energy, time and dose update with every step; a return-margin gauge and GO / NO-GO badge show when to turn back. At the target the astronaut collects an 8 kg sample and the return leg is re-planned with the heavier load. A debrief compares plan with outcome. Users can also plan their own route between any two points on the map.

**How it works.**
1. *Data layers:* slope from the USGS HiRISE DTM (1 m); rock cover from our shadow-based detector on the HiRISE orthophoto (25 cm); terrain-class probabilities (soil / bedrock / sand / big rock) and an uncertainty value from a DeepLabv3+ (ResNet34) model trained on AI4Mars and applied to 391 Curiosity Navcam frames at 128 stops.
2. *Cost model:* the Pandolf walking-energy equation with Soule-Goldman terrain factors, calibrated to the Apollo EVA average of 272 W (NASA SP-368); 20° no-go slope (NASA EVA Lunar Surface ConOps, EVA-EXP-0075) and 15° caution.
3. *Planner API (FastAPI):* three routes with per-segment reasons, re-planning around observations (`/replan`), slope-only vs terrain-model comparison (`/compare`), and an `/assumptions` endpoint that lists every parameter with its source.
4. *Simulator (Three.js):* streamed terrain tiles, life-support HUD, live route (re-planned from the current position about every 3 m or 4 s), scanner, helmet camera with Navcam direction from the NASA CAHVOR camera model and rover attitude, and an optional in-browser ONNX run of the terrain model.

**Benefits and intended impact.** A pre-EVA briefing tool for astronauts and mission planners: see the cost of a route before going outside, compare options, and know which numbers are measurements, model estimates or assumptions. Near-term use: analog missions and training.

**Validation (honest).** Terrain model on the expert-labelled AI4Mars test: mIoU 0.798, large-rock recall 0.973, pixel ECE 0.004. Our routes run on average ~130 m from Curiosity's real track between the same endpoints (geometry check only). The scanner matched 3 of 6 checked stops; both misses were sandy. Sand is our weakest point and we say so in the app.

**Tools.** Python, FastAPI, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-image, PyTorch (DeepLabv3+, U-Net), ONNX Runtime Web, Three.js, React + Leaflet, Playwright (automated tests), pytest and node:test.

## NASA data
- USGS MSL Gale Merged DEM 1 m v3 (HiRISE-based) — terrain, slope, route cost
- USGS MSL Gale Orthophoto Mosaic 25 cm v3 (HiRISE) — surface colour, rock detection
- Curiosity Navcam raw images (NASA/JPL), 391 frames, sols 4580–4848 — terrain model input, helmet camera
- NASA/JPL MMGIS Curiosity traverse and waypoints — stops, validation
- AI4Mars (NASA rover images labelled by volunteers and experts) — model training and testing
- Curiosity RAD surface dose rate (Hassler et al. 2014) — dose gauge
- MRO CRISM MTRDR products — geology context for science stops
- NASA EVA references: SP-368 (Apollo PLSS, metabolic rate), EVA-EXP-0075 (slope), NASA-STD-3001

## Use of artificial intelligence
- *As a method:* the terrain-class model is a DeepLabv3+ network trained on the AI4Mars dataset, with MC Dropout uncertainty. The rock detector is classical image processing, not machine learning.
- *As a tool:* the team used AI coding and writing assistants (Claude Code) for parts of the code, tests and documents. Team members reviewed the outputs and checked every number against its source file.
- *Not generated by AI:* NASA data, the Curiosity track, Navcam images and published equations come from their sources. The demo video's voice-over is synthesised with ElevenLabs from our own script.
